summary: Prose that was composed rather than thought through, and that reads well while saying little.

## Look for

- Lines that would still read true about a competitor (the substitution test).
- Strong verbs with no object: "drives meaningful impact", "unlocks value".
- Filler transitions and scene-setting: "it's worth noting", "in today's landscape".
- Symmetrical scaffolding: three sections of three equal bullets, whether or not the content divides that way.
- Sentences that mean nothing when you ask "so what?"

## How to tell

Run the substitution test on the paragraph. Strip it to its claims and count them. A `filler.per_1000_words` above 5 is evidence, not proof, so read the hits in context.

## Not this

Plain, specific prose you'd have phrased differently. Never claim, guess or mention who or what wrote the doc. Flag the prose, not the author.

## When it's worst

- **Stops it:** the section meant to say what the thing does says nothing.
- **Costs trust:** whole sections pass the substitution test.
- **Slows the reader:** scattered filler in otherwise specific writing.

## How it reads

"This reads well and says nothing. Swap in a competitor's name and it's still true. Name the capability and the number."

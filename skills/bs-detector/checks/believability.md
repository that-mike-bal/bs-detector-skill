---
name: believability
summary: Claims a knowledgeable reader won't believe whatever is cited, and judgment calls stated as settled.
signals: none
---

## Look for

- A result far outside anything this team, product or market has produced.
- A real source attached to a conclusion it doesn't reach.
- Everything too clean: round numbers, and every risk tidily mitigated.
- "Proven", "validated", "we know" or "clearly" on a pilot, an estimate or an open debate.
- An unexamined premise carrying the argument: "since customers obviously want X".

## How to tell

Judge it from what you know, without searching. Ask what someone who does this for a living would say on hearing it out loud. Mark it believable, a stretch, or doesn't pass.

## Not this

An unsourced claim nobody would question. A bold target that's labeled as a stretch goal.

## When it's worst

- **Stops it:** a claim the ask rests on doesn't pass. Say so even when it's cited, because a citation doesn't rescue it.
- **Costs trust:** a stretch stated as certain, or a premise that wouldn't survive one direct question.
- **Slows the reader:** settled language on a minor point.

## How it reads

"I don't buy it. No one else will either. A migration doesn't cut tickets 60% in its first quarter. It usually raises them first."

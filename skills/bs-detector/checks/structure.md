---
name: structure
summary: Whether the parts come in the order the reader needs them and add up to one argument.
signals: none
---

## Look for

- Evidence before the claim it supports, so the reader holds facts with no idea why.
- History, background or methodology ahead of the recommendation.
- Sections that make sense alone but don't connect: no "so", no "which means".
- A conclusion that introduces something the body never argued.
- Steps in an order that can't work, such as phase 2 needing phase 3's output.

## How to tell

Read only the headings and the first sentence of each section. They should tell the story on their own. If they read like the contents of several unrelated docs, the narrative is missing.

## Not this

A different order you'd have chosen that still works. Structure is a finding only when the reader gets lost, waits too long for the point, or has to connect the parts themselves.

## When it's worst

- **Stops it:** the plan's sequence can't work, or the conclusion rests on something the body never established.
- **Costs trust:** the reader has to assemble the argument themselves.
- **Slows the reader:** context comes before the claim, but the claim does arrive.

## How it reads

"You make me read three pages of history before you tell me what you want. Lead with the recommendation and move the history to an appendix."

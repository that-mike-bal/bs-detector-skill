---
name: false-blockers
summary: Things stated as blocked, required, impossible or not allowed, with nothing showing they are.
signals: none
---

## Look for

- "We can't ship until legal signs off", where nothing requires it.
- "The platform requires" or "policy says", with no policy named.
- A blocking dependency on a team that was never asked.
- A missed date blamed on a blocker that cleared earlier in the doc's own timeline.
- The mirror image: "done", "unblocked" or "approved" on something still open.

## How to tell

For each constraint, ask who or what imposes it, and whether the doc shows it. Check it against what you know about the org: memory, project notes, connected tools.

## Not this

A real constraint with a named owner or source. A risk flagged as a risk rather than stated as a wall.

## When it's worst

- **Stops it:** the ask rests on the blocker, whether that's more time, more budget, a cut or an exception.
- **Costs trust:** the plan bends around a constraint nobody has shown is real.
- **Slows the reader:** an unexplained "requires" on a side detail.

## How it reads

"You're blocked on something that isn't blocking you. Nothing ties launch to the brand refresh. Who said it was required?"
